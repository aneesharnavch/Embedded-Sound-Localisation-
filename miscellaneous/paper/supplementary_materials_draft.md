# Supplementary materials

## Confidence scores, temporal accumulation, simulation protocol, and evidence boundary for
“The reverberation bias floor of a minimal three-microphone direction-of-arrival sensor”

**Author:** Aneesh Arnav Chikkala  
**Status:** Draft supplementary material; hardware-dependent fields are intentionally empty.

This document is designed to accompany `main_paper_draft.md`. It makes the estimator, uncertainty
claims, numerical protocol, and data limitations explicit enough for an independent implementation.
It does not turn the current invalid acquisition-chain recordings into a localization experiment.

## S1. Scope, notation, and claim levels

### S1.1 Claim levels

Every numerical statement in the paper belongs to one of three evidence classes:

| Label | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **SIM** | Generated by the post-rebaseline simulation harness | free-field RMSE, RT60 floor, scaling slopes |
| **ACQ** | Measured from the available acquisition-chain recordings | ~1 kHz sample rate, idle RMS, clipping ceiling |
| **HW-PENDING** | Requires a valid hardware-timed campaign | measured DoA RMSE, real latency, sim-versus-real agreement |

The phrase “validated on hardware” must not appear unless the HW-PENDING fields have been completed.
The current defensible statement is “the acquisition chain was characterized and found insufficient
for a DoA claim.”

Angles are represented in radians internally. Reported errors are wrapped circular errors in degrees:

\[
e(\hat\theta,\theta)=
\operatorname{deg}\left(\operatorname{atan2}\left(\sin(\hat\theta-\theta),
\cos(\hat\theta-\theta)\right)\right).
\]

Using ordinary subtraction would make an estimate at (179^\circ) and a truth at (-179^\circ)
appear to differ by (358^\circ), which is a bookkeeping error rather than an angular error.

## S2. Array geometry and forward model

### S2.1 Planar far-field model

For a source at azimuth (	heta), the unit vector is

\[
u(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta\\\sin\theta\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The propagation delay at microphone (m), relative to an arbitrary origin, is

\[
\tau_m=-\frac{p_m^Tu}{c}.
\]

Only differences are observable:

\[
\tau_{ij}=-\frac{(p_i-p_j)^Tu}{c}.
\]

For pair ordering ((1,2),(1,3),(2,3)), define

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}
(p_1-p_2)^T\\
(p_1-p_3)^T\\
(p_2-p_3)^T
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
b=-c\begin{bmatrix}\tau_{12}\\\tau_{13}\\\tau_{23}\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The exact delays satisfy (Au=b), but measured delays generally violate the cycle relation and
the unit-norm relation. The estimator therefore solves an approximation and reports the residual.

### S2.2 Delay-cycle consistency

The three pairwise delays are not independent. With the stated ordering,

\[
\tau_{12}-\tau_{13}+\tau_{23}=0.
\]

Define the cycle residual

\[
\kappa=\hat\tau_{12}-\hat\tau_{13}+\hat\tau_{23}.
\]

A large (|\kappa|) indicates an internally inconsistent set of pairwise peaks, but it does not
identify the bad pair. Pairwise PSR values can rank hypotheses, yet the ranking is not a calibrated
probability. A strong reflection may have high PSR, and a low-SNR direct path may have low PSR.

### S2.3 Near-field model used only in the supplement

For a source at (s\in\mathbb R^2), the spherical-wavefront arrival time is

\[
\tau_m(s)=\frac{\|s-p_m\|}{c},
\qquad
\tau_{ij}(s)=\frac{\|s-p_i\|-\|s-p_j\|}{c}.
\]

The current quadrant tables are reproduced by a forward model and therefore belong here as an
analytic illustration. They are not measurements and must not be presented as an experimental
near-field result.

## S3. GCC-PHAT implementation

### S3.1 Windowing and transform

For each frame, subtract the channel mean, multiply by a common analysis window (h[n]), and compute

\[
X_m[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}h[n]x_m[n]e^{-2\pi i kn/N}.
\]

The same window and frame boundaries must be used for all channels. A channel-specific window or
different timestamp would create a delay-like bias.

### S3.2 Regularized PHAT

For pair (i,j),

\[
G_{ij}[k]=X_i[k]X_j^*[k],
\qquad
\Phi_{ij}[k]=m[k]\frac{G_{ij}[k]}{|G_{ij}[k]|+\epsilon},
\]

where (m[k]in\{0,1\}) is the common passband mask. The inverse transform gives the circular
correlation. If a real-valued transform is used, the conjugate-symmetric bins must be handled so
that the inverse transform is real.

The mask is part of the estimator definition. A fair comparison must use the same source band for
GCC-PHAT, SRP-PHAT, and any frequency-domain MUSIC baseline. Comparing band-limited GCC to wideband
MUSIC confounds algorithm and front-end bandwidth.

### S3.3 Physical lag interval

For pair spacing (d_{ij}=\|p_i-p_j\|), the direct-path delay must satisfy

\[
|\tau_{ij}|\leq d_{ij}/c.
\]

In sample units, the allowed interval is

\[
|\ell_{ij}|\leq f_s d_{ij}/c.
\]

The discrete search interval is the integer set inside this bound, expanded only by an explicitly
reported interpolation tolerance. At (f_s\approx 1000\,\mathrm{Hz}), (d=0.05\,\mathrm m), and
(c\approx343\,\mathrm{m/s}), the physical interval is about 0.146 sample wide. That is why the
available recordings collapse the full azimuth range into the lag-zero bin.

### S3.4 Parabolic sub-sample refinement

Let (r_{-1},r_0,r_{+1}) be the correlation values around the integer maximum. The vertex offset is

\[
\delta=\frac{1}{2}\frac{r_{-1}-r_{+1}}{r_{-1}-2r_0+r_{+1}}.
\]

The formula is used only when the denominator is numerically safe and the resulting offset lies in
the local peak interval. It cannot recover an acoustic delay smaller than the information supported
by the sampled, band-limited correlation; it only interpolates the shape already present.

## S4. Confidence and hedging mathematics

### S4.1 PSR definition

Let (r_p) be the selected correlation value and (\mathcal S) a sidelobe set. Exclude the main
peak neighbourhood from (\mathcal S); otherwise the score measures the peak against itself.

\[
\mu_s=\frac{1}{|\mathcal S|}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal S}r[\ell],
\qquad
\sigma_s^2=\frac{1}{|\mathcal S|-1}\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal S}(r[\ell]-\mu_s)^2,
\]

\[
q=\frac{r_p-\mu_s}{\sigma_s+\epsilon_q}.
\]

The score is dimensionless and useful for ranking peaks within a comparable condition. It is not a
posterior probability unless a separate calibration model is fitted on held-out data.

### S4.2 Optional score calibration

If future data support calibration, define a correctness label (y\in\{0,1\}) using a known delay
tolerance and fit a monotone calibration function on training data only:

\[
\Pr(y=1\mid q,\,\mathrm{SNR},\,d_{ij},\,B)=\sigma\bigl(\beta_0+\beta_1q+
\beta_2\mathrm{SNR}+\beta_3d_{ij}+\beta_4B\bigr),
\]

where (sigma(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})). Calibration must be evaluated with reliability diagrams and
expected calibration error. Until this exists, the paper should call (q) a score, not a confidence
probability.

### S4.3 Pair gate

Let (q_{\max}=\max_k q_k). The relative gate is

\[
g_k=\mathbf 1\{\text{physical lag valid}\}\mathbf 1\{q_k\geq\alpha q_{\max}\}.
\]

An absolute floor can be added:

\[
g_k=\mathbf 1\{\text{physical lag valid}\}\mathbf 1\{q_k\geq q_0\}
\mathbf 1\{q_k\geq\alpha q_{\max}\}.
\]

The exact values of (q_0) and (alpha) are `[HARDWARE DATA REQUIRED: implementation/configuration
record]` if they are not recoverable from the original validation code. They must be fixed before
the final evaluation and not chosen after looking at the test errors.

### S4.4 Weight families

Three useful weight families make the ablation interpretable:

1. **Uniform:** (w_k=g_k). This isolates the effect of rejection.
2. **Linear score:** (w_k=g_k\max(q_k-q_0,0)). This tests rank information without an exponential tail.
3. **Power score:** (w_k=g_k[\max(q_k-q_0,0)]^\gamma). This tests concentration and allows
   (\gamma=0) to recover the uniform survivor case.

Weights should be normalized only when comparing numerical scale; the direction solve is invariant to
common multiplication of all positive weights.

### S4.5 Weighted least squares and residual

With (b_k=-c\hat\tau_k),

\[
\tilde u=(A^TWA)^{-1}A^T Wb.
\]

The regularized form,

\[
\tilde u_\lambda=(A^TWA+\lambda I)^{-1}A^T Wb,
\]

is numerically safer when a gate leaves an ill-conditioned pair set. Normalize:

\[
\hat u=\tilde u_\lambda/\|\tilde u_\lambda\|,
\qquad
\hat\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\hat u_2,\hat u_1).
\]

The weighted residual is

\[
\rho=(A\hat u-b)^TW(A\hat u-b).
\]

The residual is a diagnostic, not a correctness guarantee. A reflection can produce a self-consistent
but biased set of pairwise delays.

### S4.6 Rank proof for the three-microphone limit

For a nondegenerate planar array, (\operatorname{rank}(A)=2). With (P=3) pair equations,

\[
P-\operatorname{rank}(A)=1.
\]

Suppose pair (k) is removed. The remaining (2\times2) matrix is generically full rank, so it
fits a two-coordinate direction exactly. Therefore the residual of the remaining system cannot
select a unique bad pair. A fourth microphone increases (P) faster than the direction dimension
and creates genuine fault-exclusion redundancy. This is why the confidence layer is a diagnostic
and modest robustness aid at (M=3), not a substitute for more spatial information.

## S5. Temporal accumulation and circular statistics

### S5.1 Complex resultant

Represent each angle as (z_t=e^{i\hat\theta_t}). The weighted resultant is

\[
Z_T=\sum_{t=1}^{T}w_tz_t=S_x+iS_y.
\]

The estimate and mean resultant length are

\[
\hat\theta_T=\arg(Z_T),
\qquad
\bar R_T=|Z_T|/\sum_t w_t.
\]

(ar R_T\) is a useful concentration diagnostic. It is not sufficient to distinguish low noise
from a consistently biased reflection.

### S5.2 Effective sample size

For independent frame errors with common variance, nonuniform weights yield

\[
T_\mathrm{eff}=\frac{(\sum_t w_t)^2}{\sum_t w_t^2}.
\]

Uniform weights give (T_\mathrm{eff}=T). If one frame receives almost all the weight,
(T_\mathrm{eff}\approx1), so nominal accumulation time does not equal statistical accumulation.

### S5.3 Bias–variance decomposition

Use the local small-angle approximation around the biased mean:

\[
\hat\theta_t=\theta+b+e_t,
\quad \mathbb E[e_t]=0,
\quad \operatorname{Var}(e_t)=\sigma_1^2.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{MSE}(T)=b^2+\sigma_1^2/T
\]

for uniform weights, or approximately

\[
\operatorname{MSE}(T_\mathrm{eff})=b^2+\sigma_1^2/T_\mathrm{eff}
\]

for weighted frames. The fit should be performed on squared circular errors or on a small-angle
projection, with the chosen convention stated. If the fitted (b^2<0) after an unconstrained fit,
that signals finite-sample noise or model mismatch; use a constrained nonnegative fit and report the
unconstrained diagnostic separately.

### S5.4 Correlated reverberant errors

The independent-frame approximation is a design law, not a guarantee. If errors have autocovariance
(gamma(h)), the variance of a uniform average is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\bar e_T)=\frac{1}{T^2}\left[T\gamma(0)+
2\sum_{h=1}^{T-1}(T-h)\gamma(h)\right].
\]

Positive correlation reduces the effective number of independent frames. A room can therefore make
the approach to the bias floor faster or slower than a white-noise (1/T) variance law, while the
floor itself remains the limiting term when the bias is persistent.

### S5.5 Moving-source constraint

If the true direction changes approximately at angular speed (omega), a rectangular accumulation
window of duration (L) introduces a motion-smearing scale of order (|\omega|L/2). A useful design
choice is to select (L) so that

\[
|\omega|L/2\lesssim\max\left(b,\,\sigma_1/\sqrt{T_\mathrm{eff}}\right).
\]

This inequality is a hedged engineering rule, not a measured optimal tracker. The final hardware
paper should report the chosen window and source-motion test rather than imply that a long window is
free.

## S6. Aperture, angular sensitivity, and sampling

### S6.1 Linearized angular sensitivity

For a single pair with projected separation (d_\parallel),

\[
\tau(\theta)=-\frac{d_\parallel(\theta)}{c},
\qquad
\left|\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial\theta}\right|
\leq\frac{d}{c}.
\]

Thus a delay uncertainty (sigma_\tau) produces the scale

\[
\sigma_\theta\sim \frac{c\sigma_\tau}{d_\parallel}.
\]

An array solve combines pair directions through the geometry matrix. For a well-spread triangle this
preserves the (1/R) dependence, but directions near a weak projected baseline can have larger local
variance. Report the full azimuth curve when isotropy is not established.

### S6.2 Quantisation and interpolation

The integer lag spacing is (1/f_s). If the allowed physical delay interval is much less than one
sample, every direction shares one integer lag. Interpolation can estimate the location of a smooth
peak when the peak shape is sampled by enough bins; it cannot create distinct bins for delays that the
sampling grid never distinguishes.

For the equilateral geometry, pair spacing is (d=\sqrt{3}R), and the maximum physical delay in
samples is

\[
n_{\max}=\frac{\sqrt{3}R f_s}{c}.
\]

An integer-lag search therefore contains (2\lfloor n_{\max}\rfloor+1) distinct candidate lags. If
the interpolated lag spacing is (1/(I f_s)), the corresponding angular quantisation standard
deviation used in this work is

\[
\sigma_{\theta,q}=\frac{c}{\sqrt{54}\,R I f_s}.
\]

Demanding (\sigma_{\theta,q}\leq\varepsilon_q) yields

\[
I f_s\geq\frac{c}{\sqrt{54}\,R\varepsilon_q}.
\]

The complete design condition is not a universal single-number cutoff; it is the maximum of the
bandwidth, quantisation, and native-lag requirements:

\[
f_s\geq\max\!\left\{
2f_2,\;
\frac{c}{\sqrt{54}\,R I\varepsilon_q},\;
\frac{\kappa c}{\sqrt{3}R}
\right\},
\qquad \kappa\approx5\text{--}10.
\]

For (R=0.05\,\mathrm{m}), the native-lag term alone is (19.8\text{--}39.6\,\mathrm{kHz}). At
(f_s=1\,\mathrm{kHz}), (n_{\max}\approx0.252), leaving only one integer lag; this cannot support
directional discrimination. Interpolation may refine a genuinely sampled correlation peak, but it
cannot restore aliased bandwidth or create physical delay information absent from the samples.

### S6.3 Cramér–Rao-style cross-check

For a known source spectrum and independent channel noise, the delay-information term for pair (ij)
has the generic form

\[
J_{\tau,ij}\propto \int_{\mathcal B}(2\pi f)^2
\frac{|S_x(f)|^2}{S_{n_i}(f)S_{n_j}(f)}\,df.
\]

The directional information then combines the pair delay derivatives:

\[
J_\theta=\sum_{ij}J_{\tau,ij}
\left(\frac{\partial\tau_{ij}}{\partial\theta}\right)^2.
\]

The scalar lower bound (\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)\geq J_\theta^{-1}) is a model-based lower
bound, not an expected measured result. Reverberation, unknown source spectrum, channel mismatch,
and estimator bias can all make the achieved error larger.

## S7. Estimator pseudocode

### S7.1 Per-frame estimator

```text
input: three channels x[1..3], geometry p[1..3], fs, c, band mask B

for pair k=(i,j) in [(1,2), (1,3), (2,3)]:
    xi <- remove_mean_and_window(x[i])
    xj <- remove_mean_and_window(x[j])
    G  <- FFT(xi) * conjugate(FFT(xj))
    Phi <- zero_outside_band(G / (abs(G) + epsilon))
    r <- IFFT(Phi)
    lag <- argmax(r over physical lag interval)
    delay[k] <- lag / fs + parabolic_refinement(r, lag) / fs
    psr[k] <- (peak(r) - sidelobe_mean(r)) / (sidelobe_std(r) + epsilon_q)
    valid[k] <- physical_lag_test(delay[k])

qmax <- max(psr[k] over valid pairs)
for pair k:
    gate[k] <- valid[k] and psr[k] >= alpha*qmax and psr[k] >= q0
    weight[k] <- gate[k] * max(psr[k] - q0, 0)^gamma

A <- pair_difference_matrix(p)
b <- -c * delay
u_tilde <- solve(A' * W * A + lambda*I, A' * W * b)
u <- u_tilde / norm(u_tilde)
theta <- atan2(u[2], u[1])
residual <- (A*u-b)' * W * (A*u-b)

return theta, pair scores, pair gates, residual
```

### S7.2 Constant-memory accumulator

```text
state: Sx = 0, Sy = 0

for each accepted frame:
    Sx <- Sx + frame_weight * cos(theta)
    Sy <- Sy + frame_weight * sin(theta)
    theta_acc <- atan2(Sy, Sx)

reset state when source identity, tracking epoch, or stationarity test changes
```

For the simplified final implementation, `frame_weight = 1` is the preferred baseline because the
measured benefit of score weighting is below 0.006° and changes sign across rooms.

## S8. Simulation protocol and fairness controls

### S8.1 Generative model

1. Choose a source azimuth uniformly over the evaluation grid.
2. Generate a band-limited source waveform.
3. Apply fractional delays implied by the array geometry.
4. Add independent noise to reach the target SNR.
5. For reverberation, convolve with a fixed image-source room response at the target RT60.
6. Apply the same frame length, window, passband, FFT convention, and peak refinement to every
   correlation baseline.
7. Evaluate wrapped angular errors and save trial-level outputs before aggregation.

### S8.2 Baseline controls

The following must match across baselines:

- microphone coordinates and sound speed;
- source band and passband mask;
- frame boundaries and window;
- delay search interval;
- trial azimuths and noise seeds;
- treatment of invalid peaks;
- interpolation factor, or a clearly labelled native/interpolated comparison;
- metric definition and aggregation order.

An estimator may use a different internal algorithm, but it must not receive a wider band, a different
geometry, or privileged ground truth.

### S8.3 Statistical reporting

For errors (e_1,\ldots,e_n), report at least median absolute error, p90, and RMSE. For RMSE,

\[
\operatorname{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^{n}e_r^2}.
\]

For independent trial-level squared errors (v_r=e_r^2), a delta-method standard error is

\[
\operatorname{SE}(\operatorname{RMSE})\approx
\frac{\operatorname{sd}(v)}{2\sqrt n\,\operatorname{RMSE}}.
\]

For paired ablations, compute (d_r=e_{r,A}-e_{r,B}) on the same trial and report

\[
\bar d\pm t_{n-1,0.975}\frac{\operatorname{sd}(d)}{\sqrt n}.
\]

The 540 matched frames cited for the gate/weight ablation make small effects statistically visible;
statistical significance must not be presented as engineering importance.

## S10. Acquisition-chain forensic record

### S10.1 What the available recordings establish

- The logged timebase clusters at approximately 1 ms and yields (f_s=999.988\,\mathrm{Hz}).
- The per-channel idle floor is 4.0–5.6 LSB RMS, approximately 25 dB above a nominal 12-bit
  quantisation floor.
- The chain clips, giving a peak-SNR ceiling of 47.9 dB under the recorded level convention.
- Broadband channel gain mismatch is approximately 1.2 dB median.
- The existing 30-sample triple-microphone clips do not contain enough simultaneous, correctly timed
  samples to recover a physical TDOA for the intended array.

### S10.2 What they do not establish

They do not establish an angular RMSE, CDF, heatmap, frequency response over 1–20 kHz, phase mismatch,
measured on-device latency, or simulation-to-hardware agreement. The previously generated synthetic
77 % accuracy heatmap must not be used.

## S14. Claim audit before submission

### Claims allowed with the current evidence

- The simulation reference configuration reaches 0.301 ± 0.006° free-field RMSE at 10 dB SNR.
- The post-rebaseline temporal curves follow the free-field (T^{-1/2}) law.
- The simulated room floors are 1.481°, 1.978°, and 2.557° at the stated RT60 values.
- PSR gating and weighting have a small matched-trial effect in the three-microphone system.
- The aperture and snapshot scaling slopes are close to (1/R) and (N^{-1/2}).
- The available acquisition chain has a sample rate of 999.988 Hz and cannot support the intended DoA claim.
- The available recordings establish electrical noise, clipping, and gain-mismatch facts in ADC units.

### Claims requiring the hardware campaign

- Any measured angular RMSE, median error, p90, or CDF.
- Any claim of agreement between simulation and physical recordings.
- Any claim of real-time latency, SRAM use, CPU load, or energy efficiency.
- Any calibrated acoustic self-noise or microphone frequency-response claim.
- Any claim that a specific board geometry is the simulated 5 cm geometry.

- Knapp and Carter (1976), GCC and PHAT weighting [knapp1976gcc].
- DiBiase (2000) and DiBiase et al. in Brandstein and Ward (2001), SRP-PHAT [dibiase2000srpphat].
- Schmidt (1986), MUSIC [schmidt1986music].
- Allen and Berkley (1979), image-source room simulation [allen1979image].
- Carter (1987), coherence and delay estimation [carter1987coherence].
- Brandstein and Silverman (1997), robust reverberant TDOA [brandstein1997robust].
- Grondin and Michaud (2019), lightweight localization/tracking [grondin2019lightweight].
- Argentieri et al. (2015), sound-source localization survey [argentieri2015survey].
- Benesty, Chen, and Huang (2008), microphone-array foundations [benesty2008microphone].
- Chan and Ho (1994), hyperbolic near-field localization [chan1994hyperbolic].
