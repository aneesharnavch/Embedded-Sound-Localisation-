Real-time sound-source localization is essential for assistive hearing systems, wearable interfaces, robots, and distributed IoT sensors. However, the computational, memory, and energy requirements of conventional localization methods, such as beamforming, limit their use in low-cost, battery-powered edge devices. These constraints motivate a deliberately minimal architecture comprising three microphones and lightweight GCC-PHAT time-delay estimation. This study investigates whether temporal averaging can improve the direction-of-arrival (DoA) accuracy of this architecture without increasing per-frame processing, while examining how simulation and observation conditions affect the measured performance. Independent numerical calculations were used to verify the direct-path delays and simulated room responses. Matched simulations then examined the effects of arrival-time rounding, source onset, and reverberation. Separate recordings provided the data for assessing temporal averaging. At an SNR of 10 dB relative to the direct sound, rounding arrival times to the nearest sample increased the direct-path root-mean-square error (RMSE) from 0.92◦ to 2.30◦ . In the more reverberant cases, the first frame after source onset gave an RMSE of 4.16◦ , compared with 42.51◦ during steady operation. The lower error at source onset occurs because the reflected sound field has not yet fully developed. Averaging 128 frames, corresponding to 5.46 s, reduced the RMSE to 0.84◦ under lower reverberation and 10.16◦ under stronger reverberation. These results demonstrate that temporal averaging can substantially improve the accuracy of a computationally minimal DoA system, although the achievable performance remains limited by the acoustic environment. The findings also highlight the importance of controlling arrival-time discretization, source timing, reverberation, and observation duration when evaluating compact localization systems.
